Tennessee short-term rental taxes — sales tax, Nashville hotel tax, and no state income tax

By Richard Srey · Updated May 2026 · 13 min read

Educational information only — not legal or tax advice. Rules change frequently; verify current requirements with your state, city, county, and a licensed CPA or attorney.

Tennessee is one of the most tax-favorable states for short-term rental operators: no state income tax (since 2022), a 7% state sales tax that's moderate by national standards, and Airbnb collecting most of the applicable taxes automatically in major markets. Nashville adds a Metro Hotel Occupancy Tax that pushes that city's combined rate to around 15%. And the Smoky Mountains market — Sevier County — is one of the most STR-dense in the Southeast, with active local enforcement to match.

⚠️ The direct answer: Tennessee charges a 7% state sales tax on short-term rental income plus local sales tax add-ons (2.25–2.75% depending on county), for a combined state-and-local rate of 9.25–9.75%. Nashville adds a Metro hotel occupancy tax on top. Tennessee has no state income tax — rental profit is subject only to federal income tax. Airbnb collects and remits Tennessee state sales tax and Nashville hotel taxes in most markets. No statewide STR license is required, but many cities and counties have their own permit requirements.

State-level registration and permitting — no statewide license, local rules vary

Tennessee has no statewide STR license program — permits and registrations are handled entirely at the local level. What the state does require is registration with the Tennessee Dept of Revenue to collect sales tax on rental income. Register through TNTAP (Tennessee Taxpayer Access Point) at tntap.tn.gov before collecting your first rental payment. Once registered, you receive a sales tax account number and begin filing state sales tax returns.

At the city and county level, requirements differ significantly. Nashville (Metro Nashville-Davidson County) requires an STR permit with two categories: owner-occupied (primary residence) and non-owner-occupied. Non-owner-occupied STRs in certain residential zones require additional zoning approval and face more restrictions. Nashville's STR ordinance has been amended multiple times since 2019 — if you registered under an older version, verify your permit is still valid under current rules. Memphis-Shelby County has its own STR registration process. Knoxville has an STR permit requirement through the City. Sevier County (covering Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge, and surrounding Smoky Mountains communities) requires local registration and HOT compliance — the county actively monitors unregistered properties given the enormous volume of STR activity in that market. Pigeon Forge has similar obligations within the same county system.

Tennessee's SB 388/HB 487 (2023) was enacted to limit local governments' ability to ban STRs outright, though cities retain substantial regulatory authority over permitting, zoning, and operational requirements. Verify the current scope of this preemption with a Tennessee attorney if your city has enacted restrictions that seem to conflict with state law.

Nashville's STR ordinance has been amended multiple times. If you registered under an older ordinance version, verify your permit is still valid under current rules.

State-level taxes — 7% sales tax and zero income tax

Tennessee charges a 7% state sales tax on short-term rental income (typically stays under 90 days). Tennessee taxes accommodations under its sales tax code — this is not a separate hotel/occupancy tax, it's the standard sales tax system applied to transient lodging. Counties add up to 2.75% on top of the 7% state rate, and some cities add additional fractions. The combined state-and-local rate runs 9.25% to 9.75% across most Tennessee markets (Sevier County, at 2.75% local add-on, is at the higher end). All state and county sales tax is collected and remitted on the same Tennessee sales tax return through TNTAP.

Tennessee has no state income tax. The Hall Income Tax — Tennessee's last remaining income tax — was eliminated entirely as of January 1, 2022. Tennessee now has zero state income tax on all income including rental income, wages, and business profits. Your entire rental profit is subject only to federal income tax, reported on Schedule E of Form 1040. This is a significant advantage that attracts real estate investors to Tennessee — no state income tax regardless of how much your rental earns, and no state tax return to file for income purposes. Tennessee has no state-level equivalent of the federal self-employment tax either.

Tennessee's zero state income tax makes it one of the most favorable states for STR operators. Combined with relatively modest local sales tax rates, Tennessee's overall tax burden on STR income is among the lowest of any major STR market in the country.

Common city and county taxes — Nashville HOT, Sevier County, and other markets

Beyond the 7% state sales tax and county add-ons, some Tennessee jurisdictions impose a separate Metro or county hotel occupancy tax on top. Nashville-Davidson County has been aggressive in collecting its Metro Occupancy Tax from STR hosts — this tax is handled by the Metro Finance Dept separately from state DOR, and Nashville hosts must register with both. Sevier County (Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge) adds a 5% county hotel tax on top of state and county sales tax, making it one of the higher-rate markets in Tennessee — verify the current rate and registration process directly with the Sevier County Finance office before listing.

What Airbnb and Vrbo collect automatically in Tennessee

Airbnb collects and remits Tennessee state sales tax (7% plus local county add-ons) for all Tennessee bookings. For Nashville bookings specifically, Airbnb also collects and remits the Metro Hotel Occupancy Tax. For Sevier County (Gatlinburg/Pigeon Forge) — verify current Airbnb coverage for county-specific hotel taxes on Airbnb's tax collection help page, as coverage can vary and the county's specific tax may require separate handling. Vrbo has strong Tennessee coverage given the size of the Gatlinburg market (one of Vrbo's strongest Southeast markets) — verify current specifics at Vrbo's tax collection page. Direct bookings are always fully the host's responsibility.

Even when Airbnb collects and remits all applicable taxes, you must maintain your Tennessee DOR sales tax registration and file as directed — including zero-liability returns as required by your assigned filing frequency. Nashville hosts also need their Metro Finance registration active and current. In the Smoky Mountains market, both Airbnb and Vrbo require registration numbers in listings — platform-required compliance makes enforcement more visible and targeted in that market.

The Smoky Mountains market (Sevier County) is one of the most competitive STR markets in the Southeast. Compliance is closely monitored — both platforms require registration numbers in listings and county authorities actively investigate.

Filing schedule and where to file

Tennessee state sales tax: file monthly if annual liability exceeds approximately $200/month average, quarterly, or annually for low-volume operators. File through TNTAP at tntap.tn.gov. Returns are due on the 20th of the month following the close of the reporting period. Nashville Metro hotel occupancy tax must be filed separately with Nashville's Metro Finance Dept — Nashville has online filing available, and most active STR hosts file monthly. Sevier County hotel tax is filed directly with the Sevier County Finance office, also monthly for active STR hosts. There is no unified Tennessee STR tax portal.

Federal income tax: annual, Schedule E on Form 1040. No Tennessee state income tax return is required — Tennessee eliminated its income tax entirely in 2022. STR permit renewal is annual in Nashville, Memphis, and most Tennessee cities. Most cities that require STR permits also require a current permit number to be displayed in listings — keep your permit renewed and your listing updated with the current number to avoid platform-enforced removal.

Penalties for non-compliance

The Tennessee DOR imposes penalties of 5% of unpaid sales tax per month, up to a 25% maximum, plus interest at 1% per month. Willful non-collection or non-remittance can trigger criminal charges under Tennessee tax law. The DOR actively cross-references platform data in markets like Nashville and Sevier County — compliance gaps that would have gone undetected a few years ago are increasingly visible to state tax authorities.

Nashville STR permit violations can result in fines and potential revocation of the STR permit. Nashville's codes enforcement actively investigates complaints and cross-references listing platforms for unregistered properties. In Sevier County, given the enormous volume of STR activity, the county finance office audits actively — the high concentration of STRs in the Gatlinburg and Pigeon Forge areas makes non-compliant operators relatively easy to identify. Airbnb removes listings in markets where permit numbers are required if valid numbers are not posted.

Recent rule changes — 2024–2026

Hall Income Tax full elimination (effective January 1, 2022): Tennessee now has zero state income tax. While this isn't a change from 2024–2026, many out-of-state investors purchasing Tennessee STR properties are still unaware of the full scope of this elimination — there is truly no Tennessee income tax on rental profit. Tennessee STR preemption (SB 388/HB 487, 2023): Limited local governments' authority to ban STRs outright in most circumstances. Cities may regulate but may not completely prohibit STR operations — tracking a similar trend to Arizona and Florida preemption laws. Local permit requirements remain fully enforceable under this framework.

Nashville STR ordinance updates (2023–2024): Nashville continued modifying its STR permit system, particularly around non-owner-occupied permits in residential zones. Verify current Nashville permit requirements and zone restrictions before listing or adding a new property in Davidson County. Smoky Mountains market growth (2023–2025): Sevier County's STR market continued explosive growth post-pandemic. The county has increased enforcement focus to ensure all new listings are registered and compliant — if listing in Sevier County, ensure registration is complete before your first booking.

The Gatlinburg/Sevier County market is heavily platform-driven. Platform-required registration numbers make compliance more visible and enforcement more targeted. If listing in Sevier County, ensure registration is complete before your first booking.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Tennessee sales tax rate on short-term rental income?

Tennessee charges 7% state sales tax on short-term rental income, plus a county/city add-on of up to 2.75% depending on location. The combined state-and-local rate ranges from 9.25% to 9.75% in most Tennessee markets. Nashville adds an additional Metro Hotel Occupancy Tax, pushing Nashville's combined rate to approximately 15.25%. Register with the Tennessee Dept of Revenue (TNTAP) to collect and remit.

Does Tennessee have a state income tax on rental income?

No. Tennessee eliminated its Hall Income Tax entirely as of January 1, 2022. Tennessee now has zero state income tax on all income sources, including rental income, wages, and business profits. All rental profit from a Tennessee STR is subject only to federal income tax (Schedule E on Form 1040). Tennessee's combination of no income tax and modest sales tax rates makes it one of the most favorable states for STR operators.

Does Airbnb collect Tennessee sales tax and Nashville hotel tax automatically?

Yes. Airbnb collects and remits Tennessee state sales tax (7% plus local add-ons) for all Tennessee bookings. For Nashville specifically, Airbnb also collects and remits the Metro Hotel Occupancy Tax. Sevier County (Gatlinburg/Pigeon Forge) — verify current Airbnb coverage for county-specific hotel taxes, as coverage can vary. Even when Airbnb collects, maintain your Tennessee DOR registration and file as required.

Do Tennessee STR hosts need a local permit or license?

It depends on your city. Nashville requires a short-term rental permit, with separate categories for owner-occupied and non-owner-occupied properties. Memphis, Knoxville, and Chattanooga have their own registration requirements. Sevier County (Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge) requires registration and local HOT registration. Tennessee's 2023 preemption law limits cities from banning STRs entirely, but local permit requirements remain valid and enforceable.

What's special about the Gatlinburg/Smoky Mountains STR market?

The Sevier County market (Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge, Wears Valley, and surrounding areas) is one of the most STR-dense markets in the Southeast — there are more STR properties than hotel rooms in some parts of the county. The county imposes its own Hotel Tax in addition to Tennessee state sales tax. Both Airbnb and Vrbo are dominant in this market and require registration numbers in listings. County finance authorities actively monitor for unregistered properties given the high STR volume.
