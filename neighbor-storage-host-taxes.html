Neighbor storage host taxes — passive income, Schedule E, and what you owe

By Richard Srey · Updated May 2026 · 5 min read

Educational information only — not legal or tax advice. Consult a CPA for your situation.

Renting out a garage, driveway, basement, or spare room for storage on Neighbor is one of the most tax-favorable side hustles available. It's passive rental income, almost always Schedule E, no self-employment tax, and the deductions — utilities, insurance, depreciation on structures — follow the same straightforward rules as residential rental.

⚠️ The direct answer: Neighbor storage income is almost always Schedule E — passive rental, no self-employment tax. Deductions work on the same space-percentage basis as home office deductions: storage area sq ft ÷ total home sq ft. The main catch for garage rentals is mixed personal/rental use, which reduces the deductible fraction of shared expenses.

Frequently asked questions

Does Neighbor.com send a 1099-K?

Yes. Neighbor processes payments and issues a 1099-K to hosts who exceed the federal $2,500 gross-payments threshold for 2025. Neighbor's service fee is deducted from payouts but owed tax is on gross rental amounts. Deduct the fee as a rental expense on Schedule E.

Is storage rental income Schedule E or Schedule C?

Schedule E for most Neighbor hosts. Renting space is passive rental income — no self-employment tax. Schedule C would apply only if you provide active services to renters (loading, organizing, transport), which is unusual for standard storage-space rental.

Can I deduct a portion of my mortgage or rent for the storage space?

Yes. Calculate the storage area as a percentage of total home square footage. That percentage of mortgage interest (owners), rent (renters), property taxes, utilities, and insurance is deductible as a rental expense on Schedule E.

What if my storage space is my garage — how does mixed use work?

If you also use the garage personally (parking, your own storage), deduct only the fraction of the garage actually rented. Document the rented area's dimensions and keep photos. Shared-access arrangements are harder to defend in an audit than a clearly demarcated exclusive-use area.

Do I owe self-employment tax on Neighbor income?

No, in most cases. Schedule E income is passive — not subject to the 15.3% self-employment tax. SE tax would apply only if you actively manage renters' belongings as a service, which crosses into commercial storage operations.
