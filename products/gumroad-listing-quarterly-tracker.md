## Description (the long body — goes in the rich text editor near the top of the listing)

```markdown
### If you missed a quarterly tax payment last year, you already know what's coming.

The IRS underpayment penalty isn't huge — but it isn't nothing. On a $15,000 tax bill, skipping all four quarters costs you about **$400–$800 in pure waste.** It's not deductible. It doesn't reduce next year's bill. It's just gone.

This system makes sure that doesn't happen.

---

### What you actually get

A pre-built Excel workbook (.xlsx) that opens cleanly in **Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets.** Seven tabs, all formulas wired up, ready to use today.

- **Start Here** — what the system does, 3-step usage, the four quarterly due dates, and the actual cost of skipping them
- **Setup** — Tax year, filing status, state, your expected income, last year's tax, and any W-2 income from a day job. Federal brackets and the SS wage base are editable so the file works every year.
- **Income Forecast** — Month-by-month projection across up to 6 income streams (DoorDash + Etsy + freelance + Airbnb + whatever). Annual total feeds into the calculator automatically.
- **Tax Calculator** — Calculates self-employment tax (12.4% SS up to wage base + 2.9% Medicare), federal income tax (bracket-by-bracket), state tax, and total annual obligation. Subtracts W-2 withholding for people who have both kinds of income.
- **Quarterly Payments** — Q1–Q4 due dates and target amounts for both federal and state. Log each payment with date, amount, and confirmation. Status column color-codes green / amber / red so you can see if you're on track.
- **Safe Harbor** — Both IRS safe-harbor tests calculated. The system tells you which one you're hitting (or how short you are) so the underpayment penalty literally cannot apply.
- **IRS Reference** — Plain-English answers to the 10 questions that get gig workers and freelancers in trouble: where to actually pay, what 1040-ES is, what happens if you miss a quarter, how to handle uneven income, what records to keep.

---

### Built for any self-employed income

- Gig drivers — DoorDash, Uber, Lyft, Grubhub, Instacart, Amazon Flex, TaskRabbit, Rover
- Airbnb / Vrbo / STR hosts subject to self-employment tax
- Etsy, eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, Shopify, and reseller-platform sellers
- Freelancers on Upwork, Fiverr, or working direct
- YouTube, Twitch, TikTok, Patreon, Substack creators
- W-2 + side hustle filers (the calculator factors in your day-job withholding so you only pay quarterlies on the difference)

If you get 1099s and the IRS hasn't been withholding for you, this is the file you've been winging without.

---

### What makes this different from a "calculator"

A calculator gives you a number once. This system does three things a calculator can't:

1. **It builds a record.** Every payment you make gets logged. By April, your numbers for Form 1040 line 26 are already done.
2. **It tracks safe harbor.** Both IRS tests, side by side, updating as you pay. No surprises in April.
3. **It works all year.** Income changes, the tracker updates. Pay extra one quarter, the dashboard shows it. State rate changes, you fix one cell.

---

### Format & delivery

- **Single .xlsx file.** ~20 KB. Instant download after checkout.
- Works in **Excel 2016+, Excel for Mac, Numbers** (with some styling differences), and **Google Sheets** — drag it into Drive, Sheets converts cleanly, every formula keeps working.
- All federal brackets, SE wage base, and standard deductions live on the Setup tab as editable values. **One file, every year — just update the constants each January.**

---

### A note on accuracy

This is an **estimator**, not a tax return. The tax calculator covers the cases that affect 95% of self-employed filers: SE tax with SS wage base cap, federal income tax through all seven brackets for Single/MFJ, standard deduction, and a flat state rate. It doesn't model: QBI deduction (worth 20% to many self-employed — talk to a CPA), itemized deductions, AMT, additional Medicare tax above $200K, or state tax brackets (it uses a flat rate). For complex situations, this gets you 90% of the way; a CPA closes the last 10%.
```
